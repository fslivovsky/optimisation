Extreme point tester

A point z ∈ P is not an extreme point if you can write z = θx + (1 − θ)y with x, y ∈ P, both different from z, and θ ∈ (0, 1). Choose a point z, then look for such x, y and θ. Here P is the running example: x1 + x2 ≤ 4, x1 ≤ 3, x2 ≤ 3, x ≥ 0.

Drag z (black), x and y (orange), or select a point and use the arrow keys. With snapping on, points jump onto an edge or corner of P when they get very close. With "y follows x" switched on, moving x or θ automatically places y on the other side of z, so the question becomes: can you keep both x and y inside P? At a corner you never can. To zoom, use the buttons or hold Ctrl (⌘ on a Mac) and scroll; drag the background to pan. On a touch screen, use two fingers to pan and pinch to zoom.
