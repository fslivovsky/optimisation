Choosing the step size

Gradient descent on f(x) = (a/2)x². Drag the starting point x(0), then press Step or Run. Zoom with Ctrl or ⌘ and the mouse wheel.

f(x(k)) − f* against k, on a logarithmic scale. A straight line means a constant factor per step.

Each step multiplies x by 1 − ta. The iterates converge when this factor lies strictly between −1 and 1, that is, for 0 < t < 2/a. Increase a with t fixed and watch the same step become too large.

f(x) = (a₁/2)x₁² + (a₂/2)x₂². Drag the starting point x(0), then press Step or Run. Zoom with Ctrl or ⌘ and the mouse wheel.

f(x(k)) − f* against k, on a logarithmic scale.

Each coordinate behaves like its own parabola, with factor 1 − t aᵢ. One step size has to work for both, so the larger curvature L = max(a₁, a₂) limits t, and the flatter direction converges slowly. The ratio L/μ of the curvatures is the condition number.
