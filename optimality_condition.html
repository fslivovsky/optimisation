The first-order optimality condition

Minimise a convex function f over the convex set S (blue). A point x ∈ S is optimal exactly when ∇f(x)T(y − x) ≥ 0 for every y ∈ S: no feasible direction goes downhill. Drag the black point around S. Drag the orange point to move the unconstrained minimiser of f.

The worst direction is always towards a corner of S, because ∇f(x)T(y − x) is linear in y. At the optimum on the boundary, −∇f(x) points out of S. If the unconstrained minimiser lies inside S, the optimum is there and ∇f = 0.
